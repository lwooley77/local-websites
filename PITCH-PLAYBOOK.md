# Pitch playbook: cold calls, walk-ins, rejections

Keep this open while you call. The business-specific line to say is in `PITCH-HOOKS.md`.
Remember: you aren't selling "a website". You're offering to fix something already broken, like a dead link, wrong hours or a missing booking button, and you already built it.

---

## The 3 goals (in order)
1. **Get 60 seconds.** Nothing else on the first ten seconds.
2. **Get them to look at the preview.** On your phone in person, or by a link they've agreed to receive.
3. **Get a next step with a date.** "I'll stop by Thursday at 2." Never end with "think about it."

You don't sell on the first call. You win by getting the look.

---

## Before every contact (30 seconds)
- Google the business name. Confirm the hook is true today (dead site, wrong hours).
- Open the demo on your phone and know where the Call button is.
- Know ONE thing from their STATUS.md (a service, their rating, their hours).
- Best times: barbershops and salons Tue-Thu, 1-3 PM. Avoid Saturdays, Mondays, and lunch. Contractors: 7-8 AM or after 4 PM.

---

## COLD CALL script

**1. Open (10 seconds). Say who you are, why you're calling, and that it's quick.**
> "Hi, is this the owner or manager? ... My name is ___, I'm local here in [Reno]. I'll be quick. I noticed [HOOK from PITCH-HOOKS.md]. Do you have 30 seconds?"

**2. If yes, one proof sentence, then the ask.**
> "I put together a free preview of a website for ___ using your public info: your hours, services and a Call button. Can I text you the link so you can look at it when you're free?"

**3. Get the OK before you send anything.** Ask "Is this number okay for texts?" Many shop numbers are landlines. If not: "What's a good email?" or "Can I stop by with it on my phone?"

**4. Close with a date.**
> "Great. I'll send it now. I'll call back Thursday afternoon to see what you think. Does that work?"

**5. After the call (within 5 minutes):** send the link and one line only:
> "Hi ___, ___ here. Here's the preview of the website for ___: [link]. Tell me anything that's wrong and I'll fix it."

**If they give you 2 minutes, add:** show the one thing they'll like. Their reviews, their hours, one-tap Call, or (Electric Sun) booking without leaving the page.

### Gatekeeper ("They're busy" / "Who's calling?")
> "No problem. I'm a local web designer. I made a preview of a site for the shop. When's a better time to catch ___? Or is there an email I can send it to?"
Get a name and a time. Never lie about who you are or why you're calling.

### Voicemail (20 seconds, say your number twice)
> "Hi, this is ___. I noticed [HOOK] and built a free preview website for ___. I'll text you the link, or you can call me at [number]. That's [number]. Thanks."

## WALK-IN script (best for barbershops and salons)
1. **Go mid-afternoon on a weekday.** If they have clients in the chair, wait or come back. Never interrupt a haircut.
2. **Open:** "Hi, are you the owner? I'm ___, I build websites for shops around [Reno]. I noticed [HOOK]. I made you a quick preview. Got 30 seconds?"
3. **Hand them your phone**, with the demo already loaded, and **stop talking for 10 seconds.** Let them scroll.
4. **Point to ONE thing:** their name, their hours, the Call button, or their own photos.
5. **Ask:** "What would you change?" People who fix things start owning it.
6. **Next step:** "Can I text you this link so you can show it to anyone you want?" Get their number or a time to come back.
7. **Leave** a business card or a text. Don't leave a pitch they can't look at again.

---

## If they say YES or "how much?"
> "It's $___ to launch, then $___ a month for hosting and small edits like prices and hours. I'd swap in your real photos first."

- Say the price once, then stop talking. Don't discount on your own.
- Be honest about what you don't have yet: photos and quotes need their OK, and prices and hours are theirs to confirm.
- Get paid before it goes live, and put the terms in writing.
- Don't promise results like "you'll get more clients". Say what it does: shows your info correctly and makes calling or booking one tap.

## REJECTION: what to say (and when to stop)

| They say | You say | Then |
|---|---|---|
| **"Not interested."** | "Totally fine. Can I ask what's the main reason? Is it the cost, or you're covered?" | Listen. Thank them. Mark the reason in your tracker. |
| **"I don't need a website. I get business from Instagram / word of mouth."** | "That makes sense, and you don't have to drop either. This just catches people who Google you and bounce. [HOOK: dead link / wrong hours]." | Offer to text the link. If no, thank them. |
| **"I already have one."** | "Great. What's the address? I'd like to look." (Then check it honestly.) | If it's real and good, leave it alone. Don't criticize it. |
| **"How much?"** | Say the price (above). | Then stop talking. |
| **"Too expensive."** | "I understand. What did you have in mind? The monthly part can't be skipped, but we can talk about the launch part." | Don't drop below a number you can live with. |
| **"My nephew / cousin does that."** | "Sure. If it falls through, the preview is yours to keep looking at." | Leave it open. Check back in 30 days. |
| **"Send me an email."** | "Happy to. What's the best address?" | Send it the same day. Follow up once. |
| **"I need to think about it."** | "Of course. What would you want to know to decide? Can I check back Friday?" | Write down the date and keep it. |
| **"Talk to my partner/boss."** | "Sure. Who is it, and when are they in?" | Come back when they're there. |
| **"Don't call again."** | "Understood, sorry to bother you." | **Stop. Mark do-not-contact. Never contact again.** |

### Rules for rejection
- **A "no" is information, not an insult.** Most of your calls will be no. Plan for 10 contacts to get 1-3 real conversations.
- **Ask for one reason, then accept it.** Don't argue, repeat, or guilt them.
- **Maximum 2 follow-ups.** One text after the call, one call back. Then move on.
- **Never pressure** with fake deadlines, "limited spots" or invented competitors.
- **Never lie** about who you are, what you've built, or what they've agreed to.
- **Respect "stop".** Contacting someone after they say no isn't just rude, it can get you in legal trouble.

---

## Rules that protect you
- Only contact businesses about their own business, using their business number. No personal numbers.
- Get a yes before you text. Unsolicited marketing texts can break the law (the TCPA). A phone call, a walk-in, or an email they asked for is safer.
- Use only public facts from the listings. Get the owner's OK before any photo or quote goes on a live site.
- Do not publish the demo publicly (search-indexed) before they say yes. Share links only with the owner.
- Keep a do-not-contact list. If a business says no, don't call again.

---

## Daily routine (about 90 minutes)
1. 10 minutes: pick 8-10 businesses. Google each one's hook.
2. 60 minutes: call or visit. Log every result.
3. 10 minutes: send the links you promised.
4. 10 minutes: write down what people said. Use it to improve the pitch.

**Tracker:** the `Called?` / `Result` / `Notes` columns in `Desktop\Leads\leads_prioritized_2.xlsx`.
Statuses: `no answer` → `voicemail` → `link sent` → `follow-up <date>` → `YES` / `no` / `do not contact`.

**Scoreboard goal for the first week:** 40 contacts, 8 conversations, 5 previews viewed, 1 yes. If you get 0 yeses, change the opener, not the demo.
