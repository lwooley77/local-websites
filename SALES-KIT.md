# Sales kit: selling demo sites to local businesses

The demo is already built before you contact them. That's the whole trick: you're not asking them to imagine a website, you're showing them theirs.

## The offer (keep it one sentence)
> "I already built you a website. If you like it, it's **$___ to launch** and **$___/month** for hosting and updates. If you don't, no hard feelings."

Suggested starting numbers (adjust after your first 3 conversations):
- **Launch:** $400–600. Includes their domain setup, their real photos swapped in, and one round of changes.
- **Monthly:** $30–50. Covers hosting, small edits (prices, hours), and keeping it online.
- **First 3 clients:** offer $250 launch in exchange for a review and letting you use them as a portfolio example. Say so honestly. Don't call it a "limited time" deal if it isn't one.
- Never build anything else for free after the demo. The demo is the free part.

Get paid before it goes live. A simple invoice from Square or Stripe is fine. In Nevada, check whether you need a state business license once you have paying clients (nvsilverflume.gov).

## Walk-in (best for salons and barbers: go mid-afternoon on a weekday, never during a rush)
1. "Hi, are you the owner? I'm ___, I build websites for shops around Reno. I noticed you don't have a site, so I made you one. Can I show you? Takes 30 seconds."
2. Hand them your phone with the demo open. **Stop talking** and let them scroll.
3. Point at one thing that's specific to them: their prices, their hours badge saying "Open now", the Call button.
4. "Right now people who find you on Google get a map pin and that's it. This gives them prices, hours and a one-tap call."
5. "If you want it live, it's $___ and I'll swap in your own photos. Want me to text you the link so you can think about it?"
6. Get their cell number to text the link. Log it.

## Phone call (contractors, or shops you can't visit)
"Hi, is this the owner of ___? This is ___, I'm local. Quick one: I noticed you don't have a website, so I built a free preview of one for you. Can I text you the link? No obligation."
If yes, text the link right away while you're still on the phone.

## Text follow-up (after they agree to get the link)
> Hi ___, it's ___ from earlier. Here's the preview site I made for ___: <link>
> Everything's from your public listings. Tell me anything that's wrong and I'll fix it. If you want it live, it's $___ to launch.

Follow up once after 3 days. If there's no answer after that, move on. Don't send a third message.

## Objections
- **"I get enough business from Instagram/Booksy."** "Totally, this doesn't replace that. Book Now goes straight to your Booksy. It just catches the people who Google you and bounce."
- **"Too expensive."** "One extra client a month covers it." (Only say this if their prices make it true.)
- **"My nephew can do it."** "For sure. If that falls through, the link's yours to keep looking at."
- **"Let me think about it."** "Of course. Can I check back Friday?" Write the date down.

## Rules that protect you
- Only contact businesses about their own business. Don't mass-text lists.
- Use only facts from their public listings. Get their OK on photos before anything goes live.
- If they say no, thank them and mark it no. Don't follow up again.

## Tracker
Use the `Called?` / `Result` / `Notes` columns in `Desktop\Leads\leads_prioritized_2.xlsx`.
Statuses: `visited` → `link sent` → `follow-up <date>` → `YES` / `no`.
Goal: 10 contacts a day. Expect most to say no. You only need a few yeses.
