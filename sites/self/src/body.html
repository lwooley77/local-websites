Why a website

"Why do I even need one?"

Fair question. These are the answers I hear most, and the straight ones.

"I get customers from Instagram and word of mouth."
Keep doing that. A website doesn't replace it, it backs it up. When someone hears your name and looks you up, they find something you control instead of a guess.
"My Google listing and Yelp page are enough."
Those pages are owned by someone else, and the information on them can be wrong, outdated or edited by anyone. Your own site is the one place you decide what's true: your hours, your prices, your services.
"I'm too small for a website."
It's the other way around. A small business is judged on a first impression, and a clear page says "this place is real and open for business" in seconds.
"People can just call me."
Many will. But plenty of people look first, check hours, see prices, then decide. A site answers those questions at 11 PM when you're closed, so the call you get is a ready customer.
"I don't have time to deal with it."
You won't have to. I build it, I keep it running, and a price or hours change is one message to me.
"What if it costs too much?"
You see the sample first, and you get a number only after that. If it's not worth it to you, you walk away having spent nothing.
Picture this

Moments that win a customer, or lose one.

Customers don't always have time to call. No statistics here, just things that happen every day, and what a good website does about them.

  1. 10:40 PM

    Someone needs a haircut before an interview tomorrow. You're closed, and they don't want to wait on a call back. They book a time on your site in thirty seconds, and you wake up to a full chair.

  2. Lunch break

    A customer has ten minutes and can't make a phone call. They check your hours and prices on your site, tap Text, and settle it before lunch is over.

  3. New in town

    Someone just moved here and searches for what you do. They pick the place whose hours, prices and photos they can see, and skip the ones they can't.

  4. A friend's tip

    A friend says "go see them." The new customer looks you up first. Your site shows the right hours and a booking button, so they come in this week instead of "sometime."

  5. No time to talk

    A homeowner at work has a leak and can't take a call. They send a quick message from your site saying what's wrong, and you call back when you're free. The job is yours before another company even picks up.

  6. After the visit

    A customer loved the visit but forgot to rebook. A quick follow-up message with a booking link brings them back before they drift away.
